# ADR-5026-A: Weapon techniques are cast into a Strike, with two rolls

A technique that enchants a weapon (a damage profile that uses the equipped weapon) is cast into one basic Strike, from the Strike's "Enchant with" row, and nowhere else in the focused slot: "I sort of see it as enchanting a strike with her weapon" (owner, 2026-11-09). It is a per-hit cast, paid through the technique's existing cast cost on every attack, hit or miss, and an overspend goes through the existing deficit and Soulfray path; nothing lasts past the attack, because cheap or long-duration combat buffs work against attrition (ruling `no-cheap-long-duration-combat-buffs`). The enchanted Strike rolls twice: the technique roll (the cast check with its technique skill and the combat pull, unopposed) sets what the enchantment adds, a to-hit line from the technique's "Strike to-hit size" valued on the to-hit lane and its own typed damage parts, while the hit roll stays the Strike's own roll on the strike skill (owner: "the technique's roll should change how many bonuses we add to the strike, while the strike skill is still used for the actual attack"). The whole Strike runs inside `use_technique` so the cost, mishap and Soulfray read the technique roll. The steel stays steel: a typed weapon profile no longer turns the weapon's damage into its type, so a flaming blade's fire meets fire weakness on its own (ruling `weapon-enchant-adds-typed-damage`). Its conditions land only when the blow lands. We rejected a lasting enchantment stored on the item or the fighter (a long-duration buff, ruled out), a separate enchantment table (a weapon technique already carries cost, power and success scaling), and one roll for both (the technique skill would then decide whether a sword lands). The plain focused slot and the waiting strike refuse a weapon technique; a Charge, a joust and a compelled rage attack still carry one on their own maneuvers, and there it deals the weapon's part as a hit of its own, once per target.
