# ADR-5280: The anima ritual is never performed alone, carries two resonance picks, and its outward part waits on the partner

The anima ritual used to be a solo roll that refilled anima, with an optional partner path (#5181) that also grew one resonance. The maintainer ruled on 2026-11-22 that the ritual is a scene with a partner and is never performed alone: every door (the persona menu, the Rituals page, the ritual's own name, the web dispatch, bare `ritual anima`) refuses it with "Name someone else to take part in your ritual." The ritual carries two resonance picks on its `RitualCheckConfig`, `primal_resonance` (how the ritual feels to her) and `outward_resonance` (a Celestial or Abyssal resonance, what it reaches for in her partner), each from the resonances she holds. The first setup is free and later changes are an attunement. The award at the roll's tier (`AnimaRitualResonanceAward`) splits between the two picks by the settled shares of their two affinities in her aura: the outward part is the award times its share of the two, rounded down, and the Primal part takes the rest, so the parts always add up and a share of 0 gets 0. A Partial Success counts as a success and pays its own tier's anima budget and award (`ritual_succeeded`, the one boundary); a Failure or Critical Failure recovers no anima, eases no Soulfray and grows no Primal resonance. A part is paid only if the performer holds that resonance, because the ritual belongs to the account and another character on it may perform it with a pick she does not hold.

The outward part reaches the partner, so the partner has a say. A player partner endorses or withholds it: nothing of the outward part is granted until they answer, and Endorse grants it through the resonance ledger once. After a failed roll the partner is still asked, and an endorsement grants `AnimaConfig.failed_endorsement_percent` (default 50) of the outward part of the Success tier's award. An NPC partner needs a GM (a `GMProfile` on the player now playing it, or a GM on the scene) and gives the outward part on the roll alone, after a success only. An unanswered question lapses when its scene finishes, including a battle that ends its backing scene, and an answer after the scene is over is refused as not waiting. The picks are locked after the free first setup, and a pick keeps feeding until she changes it even if the resonance later leaves her held list. An attunement changes picks for staff-set costs on `AnimaConfig` (anima, resonance and action points, and an optional check): it checks only the picks it changes, is refused in combat and before any picks are set (the free setup is the only way to set empty picks), spends action points as its own check, never refuses for lack of anima (a shortfall becomes Soulfray, as every other spend of anima does), and a failed roll keeps the costs and the old picks. The three migrations are expand, copy and contract: `RitualCheckConfig.resonance` is copied into the Primal or the outward pick by its affinity and then dropped, `SceneActionRequest.snapshot_resonance` keeps its column and now holds the outward pick beside the new `snapshot_primal_resonance`, and `AnimaRitualPerformance` gains `endorsement`, `outward_amount_pending`, `outward_resonance` and `performer_persona`.

**Rejected:** keeping the solo ritual as the path with no partner (it refilled anima for nothing at the table, and the ritual's point is the scene with someone); a single resonance split by nothing (the aura already says how much of a character is Primal and how much leans outward, so the split costs no new number); a priced hand edit of the picks as in #3029 (an attunement with staff-set costs and a check does the same job and keeps the change a thing that happens in play); reusing `PoseEndorsement` for the partner's answer (that row settles weekly on a pose, while this answer belongs to one ritual performance and pays at once); granting the outward part on the roll for a player partner (the partner is the one the ritual reaches for, so they say whether it took hold); and letting a GM decide an NPC partner's answer (the GM only has to be there to write the NPC's reactions, so the roll decides).
