# ADR-4418: NPC-ness is a property of the character, not of who plays it

Whether a character is a player character or an NPC decides consent prompts, PvP protection (nothing kills a player character in PvP, ADR-0023), good-sport credit, charm and coercion. The ruling follows the character: a character on the NPC shelf (`RosterType.NPC`) is a story NPC, and stays one while a GM or Guide plays it, so a played major NPC gets no consent prompt and no PvP protection, exactly as when nobody plays it. `is_story_npc` and its batched form `story_npc_sheet_ids` (`world/roster/services/activity.py`) state the rule, and `is_player_character` returns False first when it holds. GM and Staff OOC characters also sit on the NPC shelf but are a person's own characters, so a typeclass in `TYPECLASS_TO_CHARACTER_TYPE` is excluded and stays a player character. The victim side of the PvP wall is deliberately conservative: `is_pvp_protected_victim` protects any non-story-NPC victim that is puppeted or has a roster entry (including an unheld Available character and a GM or Staff OOC character), so only a story NPC, or a character with no roster entry, can be killed by a PC. We rejected keeping "held by an active tenure reads as a PC" (the #4091 rule): it made a character's protection depend on who was at the keyboard, so playing an NPC withdrew the protections a GM relies on to run a scene against it, and handing the same NPC back would silently restore them.
