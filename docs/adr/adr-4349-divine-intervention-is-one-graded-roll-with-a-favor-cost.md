# ADR-4349: Divine intervention is one graded roll with a favor cost, and the faith niche uses botch immunity

Divine intervention used to be a cutoff: at 50 favor, whenever the character fell, the being's first affordable miracle fired with no roll, the same rescue for everyone. We decided it is one graded roll, made by whoever prays (the fallen character, an ally praying for them, or the being stepping in by itself at a very high favor threshold), at the invocation difficulty. The result tier decides what happens: a backfire, a sign, a lesser answer drawn from the being's intervention pool, a rescue, or a bigger miracle. A rescue and a bigger miracle spend favor, a weaker answer leaves favor owed (earned base favor pays it first, before the earning rate), every roll sets a minimum wait, and higher favor softens the owed favor and the wait. The faith niche is not a label: a character who has built it (high favor, favor invocation modifiers) rolls easier odds, and botch immunity on the Faith check category (ADR-4386), granted by a distinction or condition tied to active devotion, keeps their botch from being a backfire. The roll belongs to the one praying, who uses their own favor and modifiers and pays the cost; a prayer for another character of the same account is refused. We rejected the guaranteed trigger at a favor threshold (it is the flat outcome the owner ruled out); a Chosen-only roll bonus (the niche comes from what the character built); covenant vow perks for faith (vows are class roles, not faith); a sign with no aid as a lesser answer (it reads as a failure, so it is one); and a pledge as the price (a character holds one open pledge, and a pledge pays a reward).
