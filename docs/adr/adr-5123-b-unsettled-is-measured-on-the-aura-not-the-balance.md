# ADR-5123-B: A character is unsettled when a non-main affinity grows past who they are, measured on the aura and not on spendable balance

> Amended by #4751: the range before Unsettled (`unsettled_tolerance_percent`) and the step (`unsettled_step_percent`) are set per main affinity on `Affinity`, falling back to the `DriftConfig` values (ADR-4751); the old Rite of Atonement is removed, so `_move_partial` named below is gone.

> Amended by #5124: the two exits here are now taken only through the rite of settling, over what the character reflected on (atoning is disowning the unsettling resonance, accepting is embracing it); nothing settles with a click, and the chance to atone no longer lapses with the scene.

Drift (#4732) counted spendable balance held outside the affinity she acts with, weighted per affinity pair (`AffinityInteraction.conflict_weight`), so a character was penalised for holding a second resonance at all, and spending the balance made the penalty vanish. A mixed character is now the normal case, and the only thing that unsettles them is becoming something they are not. **Who the character is** is `CharacterAuraShare.settled_percent` per affinity, set at creation (the player's weighted picks, with the Glimpse nudge) and moved by accepting; the main affinity is the largest settled share, a tie to the lowest affinity id. `drift_for(sheet)` reads the **true aura**, not the acting aura (a worn alternate self does not change who they are): for every affinity but the main one, the excess is its own share of lifetime resonance less its settled share less `unsettled_tolerance_percent`, and `steps_past` is that excess in `unsettled_step_percent` bands, rounded up, so the focus check and Resonance Conflict keep working unchanged. Lifetime and not balance is the point: spending a streak does not settle it, a streak that fades from living on the main affinity fades silently because the settled share never shrinks, and growing the main affinity (a new resonance in it included) can never unsettle. A pull outside the main affinity calls for no focus check of its own (`off_aura_pull_steps` is gone), and a mixed character casts at the full alignment bonus in every affinity they are settled in. A Sineater's burden is stored, not derived: `CharacterResonance.mire_lifetime` is the part of lifetime taken from the Mire, written on the Sineater's grant only and moved in proportion by every conversion; it reads as the Mire share, shown apart from their own, against its own `mire_allowance_percent`. The player word is **Unsettled**, with two exits that both benefit the character: atoning (in a live scene, opened by a deed in the main affinity; it converts the excess, the Mire's part first, moving lifetime and spending balance only as far as it exists, so it works after the excess was spent) and `accept_aura_change` (their whole current aura becomes who they are, the Mire becomes their own, no corruption of its own).

Production disposition under ADR-0237: `threshold_percent`, `threshold_floor`, `severity_step`, `off_aura_pull_steps` and `conflict_weight` are a deliberate discard of tuning numbers on a staff config singleton and one staff-authored weight column, replaced by new fields in the new units, and no play-state data was carried (no threads or real characters existed). Amends the drift record in `docs/roadmap/magic.md` (#4731-#4734); ADR-0134 (a compromising act grants ordinary spendable non-native resonance) still holds.

**Rejected:** keeping balance-based drift and adding a second resonance only to threads (a mixed character would still be punished for holding what they earned); named weight tiers for creation ("mostly", "a streak"), so the player spreads points and the aura follows; a settled share that shrinks with the aura (a faded streak could never return without unsettling them); deriving the Mire from the grant ledger (the Sinner's and the Sineater's Sineating grants share one source with no role column, and conversions write no ledger rows, so the figure would drift from the truth); and forcing an exit on the player (accept and atone are both choices).
