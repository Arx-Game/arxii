# ADR-4462: An item's meaning is read from records the game keeps, never from its price

Sacrifices and ceremony offerings paid favor, worship pool and prestige by the item's template value, so a gilt mirror bought that morning earned forty times what a scarf carried for months earned, and the cheapest way to feed a being was to buy and give up. We decided that no worship, favor or ceremony payout reads a price, a fee or a material cost. One reader, `item_meaning` (`world/items/services/meaning.py`), turns records the game already keeps (when the holder got the item and how: `ItemInstance.held_since` and `holding_event`, written by `set_item_holder`; its ownership history; its crafter and any crafting-service sale; the pair tier between holder and maker, `pair_tier` in `world/relationships/helpers.py`) into meaning points, and every use asks it: a sacrifice, a ceremony offering and, later, a worn piece and the GM's detail view. A sacrifice is then a check, not a payment: the points ease the invocation check's difficulty, the outcome tier selects a `FavorOutcomeAward` of kind SACRIFICE, and a per-week limit bounds how many items count. A given-up item is soft-destroyed with its design kept (name, description, silhouette, styles, facets, designer), and the purge spares any item with a design. We rejected a new item-meaning table ("item X stands for Y to Z"): most signals already exist as typed records, so one table would copy them and drift, and the few that no record holds (a dedication, what a piece was worked from, a player's own keepsake, a requested item) are added as their own small records in the later slices, not folded into a generic table. We also rejected dividing by a bigger number or paying a flat amount per item, which rewards giving up large numbers of cheap, meaningless things.
