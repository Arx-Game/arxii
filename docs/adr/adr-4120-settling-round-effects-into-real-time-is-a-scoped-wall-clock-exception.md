# Round-counted effects settle into real time when combat ends, a scoped exception to "never wall-clock"

**Status:** accepted (2026-10-04, #4120). Related: ADR-0004 (tempo is action-driven, never wall-clock), ADR-0023 (PvP is structurally non-lethal), ADR-0049 (acute peril resolves through a guarded pool), ADR-4091 (won-over is derived allegiance; Decisions 17, 18, 21).

**Decision.** A `ROUNDS` condition only counts down inside combat, so one that outlives its fight froze forever, and the lapse sweep for a charm never saw it. When combat ends, `settle_round_effects` converts each such row on the encounter's participants and opponents (players and persistent NPCs alike) into a real-time expiry, `expires_at = now + rounds * SettleConfig.settled_seconds_per_round`, with the round counter cleared. From then on a one-minute cron (`conditions.settled_effects`) turns its harm into slow ticks paced in minutes per round (a player's turn takes minutes, not seconds), applied only through `apply_clamped_chronic_damage`, which never calls `process_damage_consequences` and keeps health strictly above the knockout floor: a settled effect cannot wound, down or kill, whether or not the player is present, and no is_afk flag is consulted. A lapsing allegiance hold with a played charmer warns that charmer once (`lapse_warned_at`, within `SettleConfig.lapse_warning_seconds` of expiry) and at lapse auto-rolls the break check at the hold's strength (a placeholder difficulty), ending through the same pool as any other ending; it opens no encounter, and whether a lapsed NPC turns hostile stays #4121's question. Acute perils (Bleeding Out, Plummeting) keep their scene-round hand-off unchanged. This is a deliberate, scoped exception to ADR-0004's "never wall-clock", justified because the converted harm is capped non-lethal, the same reason ADR-0004 lets the long-term tier advance unattended; it supersedes ADR-4091 Decisions 17 and 18, and the "no roll at lapse" clause of Decision 21, only for a hold with a played charmer (the no-fight rule of Decision 21 stands).

**Why.** Rounds are the only clock these effects had, and combat is the only place rounds run, so every poison, burn or charm left standing at the end of a fight was either permanent or invisible to the sweeps. Real time is the only clock left once the fight is over, and the clamp keeps the ADR-0004 promise (nothing harmful happens to an absent player) by capping the harm rather than by refusing to run the clock.

**Rejected.** Settling every remaining round at once (a fight's last poison becomes one lump of damage the instant it ends); freezing the row until the next combat (the status quo: permanent effects, and a charm that never lapses); handing every harm to a scene round (acute perils already work that way, but a slow poison has no player action to wait on and would stall behind scenes nobody starts); a per-hazard authored AFK action (every hazard re-authored for a state the clamp already makes safe); pausing the clock while a player is AFK (needs an is_afk signal nothing else consumes, and is moot once the harm cannot kill).

**Consequences.** Audit finding, recorded for the trail rather than as a defect: Plummeting cannot kill a present character who does not declare, because its source is environmental and the guarded pool excludes `die` for that source (ADR-0049, ADR-0023). NPC-sourced bleed-out terminal and abandonment-grace deaths on an already-downed victim remain possible, are driven by a hostile acting, and are unchanged by this decision. `SettleConfig` is a REQUIRED dashboard row; the auto-roll's difficulty and the player-facing lines are placeholders for staff to tune.
