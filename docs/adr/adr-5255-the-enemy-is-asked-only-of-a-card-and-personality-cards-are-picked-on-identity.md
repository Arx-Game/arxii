# ADR-5255: The enemy is asked only of a card that starts with one, and personality cards are picked on Identity

ADR-0279 put one priced enemy on Final Touches for every draft, under the heading "Who wants you to fail". The owner, looking at that stage on production on 2026-11-21: "the whole enemy thing comes at players making their characters too cold, feels like a non-sequitur that wouldn't be applicable to many PCs". The same look found the personality cards gone. Since ADR-0280 retired the Distinctions stage a card reaches a player only through an offer line, and the only place built for a personality card was under the three Actor's Sheet questions (ADR-0282), which a card such as Indolent or Tireless does not answer. Production held 15 active Personality cards: eight offered only after one Glimpse answer and seven offered nowhere.

Asked how deep the enemy cut should go, the owner chose to keep it and hide it: "We shouldn't cut it all, we should just make it invisible unless someone has selected a distinction where they start with an enemy ... much like the secret identity type distinctions". So the enemy is a question a held card asks, the same shape as Jailbird's crime and Presumed Dead's door: `FinalTouchesPrompt.ENEMY`, set by staff on the card (`Distinction.final_touches_prompt`). The page shows the section only then, headed by the card's name. On the server one function reads the saved enemy (`enemies.enemy_answer`), and it returns nothing while no held card asks, so the price, the reason and degree offers and the finalize write all stop together. The saved answer is kept on the draft, as a crime's is, and counts again when a card asks for it. ADR-0279's scales, prices, degree marks and collection are unchanged. This change adds the switch and no card: which cards carry an enemy is the owner's content.

Asked where a player picks personality cards, the owner chose the Identity stage. `OfferChapter.IDENTITY` is a chapter that is its own opener: a line there sets no opener field and shows to every draft. Two rules follow from that and the model enforces both: a line there is always a choice, since a bundled line open to everyone would hand the card to every character, and a card has one line there, since nothing would tell two apart. The stage draws the lines as the life page's cards, four at rest and the rest under "See N more", or every card under its heading once staff group the lines. The fifteen cards production holds are placed by staff, not by a migration: the Distinction list gains an "Offer on the Identity stage" action, for superusers as the Builder and the paste are, that adds a line for every selected card in one step, credited to the operator and logged, and the Required content page flags the stage while it has none.

*Rejected:* removing the enemy system (the section, `CharacterEnemy`, the Beginning offers, the reasons). The owner declined it; production held no enemy rows, so nothing forced the choice either way. *Rejected:* a sidecar grant model for the enemy, like the persona and organization grants. Those sidecars carry data of their own (a prestige amount, an organization); an enemy card carries none, so the staff-set prompt that Jailbird already uses is enough. *Rejected:* clearing `draft_data.enemy` when the card is let go. A card clicked off by mistake would lose a written answer, and every other card-asked answer is kept the same way. *Rejected:* placing personality cards under the three questions or in an Appearance section. Most of them answer none of the questions and none is about looks. *Rejected:* an authored section model for Identity like `AppearanceSection`. The offer line's existing `group` heading does the same job with no new table. *Rejected:* a data migration that places the fifteen cards. An offer line is authored content, and a migration may not create content rows (the seed-data rule, ADR-0013); which cards belong on the stage is also the owner's call, and several existing cards are dated. *Rejected:* placing them one at a time on the Distinction Builder only. It is fifteen identical rows whose only content is "this card, here", so the list action does it in one step.
