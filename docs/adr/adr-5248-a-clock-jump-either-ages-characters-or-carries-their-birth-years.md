# ADR-5248: A clock jump either ages characters or carries their birth years with it

When staff set the game clock's date (`game_clock.services.set_clock`), the change is a jump, and the caller must say what it does to characters (`AgeHandling`). **Aged**: birth years stay, so every chronological age (`IC year - ic_birth_year`) moves by the calendar years jumped, and the birthday task matures each character by the birthdays skipped. **Kept**: every `ic_birth_year` moves by the calendar years jumped, so no age changes, and every task's in-game last-run stamp is set to the new time. A change to an earlier date is always Kept: maturation is recorded and does not go down, so a calendar cannot make anyone younger. In both modes a jump never kills anyone by itself: every open dying window (`CharacterVitals.aging_death_ic_deadline`) moves by the length of the jump, and so does every event still to come. `GameClockHistory` records the kind of change and the birth-year shift, so a Kept jump can be undone by hand.

The owner named the uses that forced the choice (2026-11-21): a time skip of twenty years for a season where everyone plays their characters' children, where characters must age; and jumping around in continuity, a flashback season or a corrected year, where they must not. Before this, every forward jump aged everyone, and every backward jump left chronological ages lower than recorded maturation and stalled the three in-game-interval aging tasks until the calendar passed the old date again (`task_registry._is_task_due`).

*Rejected:* one behaviour for every jump. Always aging makes a calendar correction rewrite every character's age; never aging makes a generation skip a hand edit of every sheet. *Rejected:* storing ages and deriving birth years, so that a jump touches nothing. Age then stops following the calendar in ordinary play, where one game year passes every four real months, and every reader of `ic_birth_year` (creation's stamp, the heritage ceiling, the sheet) would change. *Rejected:* letting a long jump resolve dying windows and run the old-age check once per skipped month. A staff control would then kill player characters in bulk as a side effect; who died during a skipped gap is a story decision staff make by hand.

The cost: a Kept jump rewrites a play-state column on every character in one transaction, and after a change to an earlier date a birthday that falls between the new date and the old one happens again. Things timed in real time (condition expiry, letters in transit) are not touched by a jump in either mode, and population growth and sun exposure do not yet see one at all (#5250).
