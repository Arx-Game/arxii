# ADR-4512: The designer is whoever wrote the words, a dedication is permanent, and a fee buys neither

A commission stamped its payer as the piece's designer whether or not the payer wrote anything, so the person credited was not the person who did the creative work, and a payment could buy a meaning signal. We decided that `ItemInstance.designer_character_sheet` is stamped only when that character writes a name or description (at making, on a finishing pass, or on a commission), that a commissioner may write a field only where the item has none or they are already its designer, and that a fee, a price or a material cost is read by no signal. A dedication (`dedicated_to_persona`, `dedicated_by_persona`, `dedicated_at`) is written once by the maker at making or the commissioner on a commission, never to oneself, and never changed. What a piece was worked from is an `ItemLineage` row (a table, so a later design can allow more than one source) whose points are a snapshot of what the source meant to the crafter when it was used up. The credits outlive the object: a given-up or destroyed piece stays listed in examine, marked gone, and the purge and `differs_from_template` treat a dedication and a lineage as part of the design. We rejected stamping the designer on every commission (it pays the artist's customer for the artist's work), a movable dedication (a gift can be re-given, a dedication is a fact about the making), and a self-dedication (a commission for yourself already counts through your design and the artist's tie).
